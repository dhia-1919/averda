      Heat stress

      Cooling that lasts to the next break, without ice.

      A phase-change cooling vest we designed for motorcycle couriers in Dubai. It never went into production. Adapted for street crews, we think it is worth testing with yours.

      Designed and evaluated. Not produced or field-tested.

        How a phase-change pad works

        Ice is coldest the moment it goes on and fades fast. A phase-change pad melts at one fixed temperature and holds it until it has fully melted, so the cooling is steady rather than a shock that fades.

        Solid pads go in

        Four bio-based pads per set: two over the upper chest, two across the upper back.

        They melt slowly, absorbing heat

        While melting, each pad stays at its set temperature and draws heat from the body and the air around it.

        They recharge in a fridge

        About 30 minutes in a fridge turns a set solid again. Cool water or an air-conditioned room also work, more slowly. No freezer needed.

        The melting point decides the shift

        A colder pad feels better and runs out sooner. Pick a grade and a shift length to see how many pad changes a crew member would need at 50°C.

        A day on the route

        Couriers never stop in the same place twice, which made recharging the hard part. Street crews start and break at fixed points, so pads can recharge where people already are.

        Shift start

        Collect a charged set from the depot fridge. Pads in, vest on.

        Mid-shift

        Swap to the second set from a cool box on the supervisor’s van. The used set goes into the box.

        Midday break

        Sets recharge in the rest-area fridge while crews rest in the shade.

        Back on the route

        A fresh set for the second half. Pads return to the depot at shift end; the vest stays personal.

        What changes for street crews

        The courier design is a starting point, not the answer. These are the changes we would make before a single sample reaches your crews.

        Non-negotiable

        Visibility

        No dark layer over hi-vis. Either a fluorescent shell with reflective tape, or a slim vest worn under the hi-vis top. EN ISO 20471 certification would be tested before any roll-out.

        Workload

        Sweeping and lifting make more body heat than riding, so pads melt faster. Expect shorter hold times than the courier estimates until the trial measures them.

        Recharge points

        A fridge at the depot and a cool box on the supervisor’s van. Both fit how crews already work.

        Fit and hygiene

        One vest per worker, sized to them, with a washable shell. Pads are wiped and rotated, never shared mid-shift.

        Weight

        Every 100 g counts over a long shift. We would test the lightest pad mix that still lasts to the next swap.

        Where it stands, and how we would test it

        Where it stands

        We designed this vest for motorcycle couriers in Dubai in 2026: material selection, pad layout and the daily recharge cycle. It did not go into production and has not been field-tested.

        The open question for couriers was where to recharge pads on the road. Crews who start and break at fixed depots do not have that problem.

        What the trial would measure

        • How long a set holds on a real route
        • Pad changes needed per shift
        • Comfort, rated by crews after every shift
        • Any skin, fit or chafing issues
        • Whether crews keep it on through the shift
        Step 1

        Adapt

        Hi-vis shell or under-layer, sizing and pad layout for work on foot.

        Step 2

        Sample

        5 to 10 vests, with pads in two grades: 29°C and 32°C.

        Step 3

        Trial in real heat

        One depot, the first hot weeks of the year. Crews rate it; supervisors log holds and swaps.

        Step 4

        Decide together

        Volume only if crews keep wearing it. If they don’t, we stop there.

        Adapting and sampling take months, not weeks, so the realistic test window is the first hot weeks of 2027. Before samples ship, we agree an indicative price for volume, so a good trial turns straight into a decision.
